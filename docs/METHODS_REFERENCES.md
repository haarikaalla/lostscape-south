# Methods references

These references motivate evaluation design; they do not validate LostScape-South results.

- Comer, J. A., Comer, D. C., Dumitru, I. A., Priebe, C. E., & Patsolic, J. L. (2023). “Sampling methods for archaeological predictive modeling: Spatial autocorrelation and model performance.” *Journal of Archaeological Science: Reports*, 47, 103824. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jasrep.2022.103824 — supports task-specific spatial sampling and leakage-aware evaluation.
- Verhagen, P., & Whitley, T. G. (2012). “Integrating Archaeological Theory and Predictive Modeling: a Live Report from the Scene.” *Journal of Archaeological Method and Theory*, 19, 49–100. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10816-011-9102-7 — motivates explicit treatment of observational and development-driven survey bias.
- Mesía-Montenegro, C., & Sanchez-Borjas, A. (2026). “Probabilistic forecasting reveals a silent collapse of archaeological heritage in Peru’s Chillón Valley.” *Journal of Cultural Heritage*, 78, 187–197. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.culher.2026.02.004 — demonstrates that defensible disappearance forecasting requires longitudinal site-footprint loss labels and propagated uncertainty. LostScape-South lacks those labels and therefore forecasts construction pressure only.
- Agapiou, A. et al. (2024). “A novel machine learning automated change detection tool for monitoring disturbances and threats to archaeological sites.” *Remote Sensing Applications: Society and Environment*, 101396. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rsase.2024.101396 — motivates multi-date disturbance monitoring and validation against known threats.
- USGS Landsat Collection 2 documentation: https://www.usgs.gov/landsat-missions/landsat-collections — authoritative source for the historical optical archive used in pilot trend analysis.

The repository does not copy results, thresholds or claimed performance from these studies. Every LostScape-South number is generated from a tracked result table.
